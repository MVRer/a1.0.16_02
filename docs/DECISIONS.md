| D-058 | Multiplayer, one shared story (supersedes D-002). Every **participant** is a subject: an online player who is alive and not a spectator. | D-059 | Each player gets the solo pace. The director keeps separate pacing memory for each player: join grace, empty sessions, cooldowns and hard limits (15 real minutes between minors, at most 1 major per real hour), tension and its forced quiet, pity, the fake rhythm and the no-repeat history. All of it is measured in that player's own online play ticks (`GameClock.playTicks(server, player)`). Stages move on the world play clock (ticks while at least one participant is online), so there is one stage for everyone. A card fires for the player whose pacing picked it. Once-per-world things stay once per world, whoever gets there first: signature cards, still burning (D-004), the house copy (D-005), the cross row, "Stop." and F04. | Mariano (2026-10-14) chose the solo pace for each player over one pace shared by the group. |
| D-060 | Shared moments. What he does to one player reaches every participant who would naturally notice it, so two players standing together feel the same thing. (1) A sound he makes at a position plays to every player within hearing range of it in that dimension, not only to the target (`SoundCues.playAt`). (2) Fog surges and silences reach every participant within `sharedMomentRadius` blocks (default 48, same dimension) of the target, with the same timing. (3) A fired card counts as felt by every participant within `sharedMomentRadius` of the target. It goes into their pacing memory too (cooldowns, hard limits, tension), so being together never doubles what anyone feels. (4) The figure is one entity that everyone can see. A sighting set up for one player must be out of view of every player when he spawns and when he goes. Anyone's stare counts. | Follows from D-059: without it, two friends side by side would each get their own events and feel twice the solo rate. A sound only one of them hears would also break "feeling it the same". |
| D-061 | Personal records are per player: first block, first crafting table, first chest, base (respawn point, else their own first block), stillness, combat, join time, play ticks, sleeping and render distance. Text that names "you" names the player reading it, and nothing ever names someone who never joined. When a once-per-world thing needs one player's record (the house copy's house, a fragment that points at "your" first chest, the cairn's first block), it picks one player by a fixed rule and stores the choice, so it never flips between players. Default rule: the online participant with the most play ticks at that moment, ties going to the earliest join. | Per-player facts stay true for each player. Storing the choice prevents a structure or fragment from switching owner halfway through. |
| D-062 | Multiplayer robustness rules (the reviewer checks every one). (1) Never keep a `ServerPlayer` across ticks or events. Keep the UUID and look the player up again: the object changes on respawn and on rejoin. If the lookup misses, or finds someone who is no longer a participant, skip quietly. (2) Every per-player value is keyed by UUID. No field holds "the player's" anything. (3) Every once-per-world action is guarded by persisted state set in the same tick it happens, so two players in one tick can't both do it. (4) Joins and leaves are per player. One player leaving never ends another player's session; play time stops only when the last participant leaves. (5) A player who leaves, dies or becomes a spectator in the middle of something: whatever was running for them finishes or stops cleanly, out of view. The figure leaves, an armed trap stays deniable for whoever sets it off, and a scene keeps going if another participant is present. (6) Use the target player's own level. Players can be in different dimensions at the same time. (7) Spectators are never targeted, but they still count as viewers for every out-of-view rule. (8) Old worlds load: a save with the old single `subject` and `firstBlocks` turns them into that player's records. (9) Debug commands act on the player who runs them, or on a named player. From the console they act on the first participant. | Mariano (2026-10-14): "make sure you dont glitch stuff bc its two players". |
| D-063 | Deaths and hardcore with friends. Marked deaths stay one shared list, and each death records whose it was. Each player has their own line on the list. The world's story ends with Ending B only when no participant is left in it. A player who dies an ordinary death in hardcore is simply out; the others go on. | Keeps "one marked death ends the story" true for the person it happens to, without ending a friend's game in the same instant. |

Ending rules must be ones a group can actually finish, never blocked forever by a friend who is offline. Nothing breaks if someone joins or leaves halfway through. | Follows from D-058. |

| D-069 | Group pacing and the group's homes, settled in MP-8 from the MP-7 review.
