# a1.0.16\_02: Mod Design Doc

Oct 9, 2026 · @Mariano

## Vision

You are supposed to be alone in a singleplayer world, and you are not. The mod never tells you what is going on. It only leaves evidence. And he is not a sad ghost: he is angry, he wants you dead, and the lore that makes you pity him is bait.

- **The world itself is wrong.** The dread does not wait for the player to do something. Before any event fires, the land already carries scars: stripped mountains, abandoned builds, crosses, lights where nobody should be. The player can never tell if it was always like that.
- **No jumpscares.** He never appears in front of the camera, never strikes. He gets you killed with accidents that look like the game's fault, and you only find the clue afterward.
- **Ambiguity first.** Every event should be explainable as a glitch, worldgen or your own memory, at least the first time. Real events are mixed with false ones (a cow that is only a cow).
- **Never the same run.** A director decides what happens, when, and when nothing happens for days. Two players never get the same order, the same scars or the same pacing.
- **Silence is the loudest event.** Long quiet stretches are designed, not empty. They are what make the next small thing land.
- **He takes; others left things.** He only removes or moves what already exists: stone, leaves, light, your bed, a mob into a closed room. What gets added to the world was left by the people before you, the ones on the list.
- **Lore you find, not lore you are told.** Fragments are spread across the map. Each piece is ambiguous alone; together they form one arc.
- **2010 canon is fixed.** Everything we invent fills a gap and never contradicts it. Inventions are tagged \[ours\].

**Guardrails the mod never breaks**

1. He never strikes directly and is never on screen during a death. Every death he causes must look like something the game could have done on its own, and leave a clue a careful player can find afterward.
2. He never speaks or writes. Every text in the world was written by a human. He only decides which ones survive and where they turn up.
3. Default Steve, blank white eyes, no glow, no name tag.
4. When seen, he stares, then runs into the fog. Never in front of the camera.
5. His death is never explained. The developer's account always stops before it.
6. In-world, no real person, company or forum is named. Only "the developer", "the studio", "the team". Real quotes appear unattributed. These are fictional characters, not claims about real people.
7. He only removes or moves what already exists: blocks, light, items, mobs. He never creates a block or spawns a mob; darkness does that for him. Pyramids and crosses are moved material. Builds and lights were left by others.
8. Every fragment is ambiguous alone. No single piece explains the theme.
9. Never confirm who sent "Stop." or who deleted the threads.
10. The brother never has a voice. No fragment is written by him. He is pitiable only in what people wrote about him, never in what he does.
11. Containment never fully works. Silence slows him; nothing stops him.
12. Seed and version string stay exactly as above.

## The story

He is what is left of a person after the record of him was taken away, and what is left is angry. This is the writers' secret. The player only ever sees pieces of it.

### Who he was \[ours\]

The developer built the world with his brother. The developer wrote everything that puts things in. The brother wrote the passes that decide what isn't there: the carving, the clearing, the code that walks through stone and takes some of it out. When the brother was gone, his code stayed and kept running. The 2x2 tunnels, stripped trees and blanked signs are his pass, still running, with nobody left to tell it to stop.

The sand pyramids are the only thing he places. They are cairns built from what he took, and the one time he touches his brother's half of the code.

When the brother was alive, his pass had exceptions. It left the block someone was standing on. It left the light near a person. Those exceptions were never code; they were him being careful. When he was gone, the pass kept running and the care stopped. A player is just one more thing in the world that can be removed.

### Why he is angry \[ours\]

Every step of his story is a removal done to him, and each one made him angrier:

- He was made to be the part that takes away, then blamed when it did.
- Whatever happened to him took him out of the world. It is never explained.
- The team patched him out, release after release.
- His brother said he never existed.
- He was forgotten.

He is not angry at one person. He is angry that anything gets made at all. Every new world fills up with the developer's additions, and he has to come with it. Players are the developer's additions that move, and builders most of all.

### How he works \[ours\]

He cannot create and he cannot write. He removes, or moves what already exists. A cross is built from blocks taken from the spot where you died. The copy of your house is built from blocks that went missing from your real one, a few at a time. A creeper inside your locked house was moved there, or the torch that kept the room lit was taken for one night. This rule keeps every death deniable.

### Why the death was hidden \[ours\]

Two reasons, and each made the other worse. The first was grief: the developer didn't want it to become something people talked about. The second was something the team noticed. Whenever his name came up in notes, chat or a test world, the next build had tunnels nobody wrote. Talking about him seemed to put him back, so silence became containment.

### Why the team fought to keep him out \[ours, built on canon\]

Every "Removed Herobrine" line is a real containment step. They pulled the pass, shipped the build, and the traces came back, so the line had to be written again next release. You can't remove the code that does the removing.

The arc has three beats, all from the record:

1. **Acknowledged.** "I did, but he is no longer with us." The one time the developer told the truth.
2. **Denied.** "I never had a brother." The biggest removal: off the record, not just out of the code. This is what turned him into a process with no person behind it, belonging nowhere, so he can turn up anywhere.
3. **Removed.** Repeated every release, never holding.

### Why silence, and what "gone" means \[ours\]

The team's silence was not cruelty. Naming him gives him someone to aim at, and attention is how his pass finds people. Silence was the only thing that slowed him, and they knew it. "I never had a brother" was the last and most expensive containment step. It partly worked, and it cost the developer his brother for good.

"Stop being on the list" means died. Every "gone" on the list was an accident, and the crosses in the world mark where those accidents happened.

### Who wrote what \[ours\]

Every fragment was written by a human: the developer, the team, or someone on the list. None of them is fake. He decides which ones survive and where you find them. He can remove whole books, so any book still sitting in the world is there because he left it.

The cut-off endings (F02, F09, F17) are his edits. He removed the warnings and left the grief. Grief is bait: people who feel sorry for him come closer, go looking for him, and say his name.

"Stop." was the team's agreed warning to anyone who posted (F19). But a sign can be moved and a message passed on. Either the team is trying to save you, or he is handing you their own word as a taunt. We never decide which. The rule for the mod: the words are always human; where they turn up is always him.

### The lure \[ours\]

The fragments are laid out so the player slowly builds a theory that he is wounded and can be calmed. Every calming act gets the player to come back, stand still, or pay attention, and every one is a trap.

| What the player tries | Why it feels right | How it kills them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obeying "Stop." | The world goes quiet and vanilla again | False peace. You get comfortable, place fewer torches, take shortcuts. See Ending A |
| Leaving offerings at a cairn | The items vanish, as if accepted | You come back to check along the same route. By the third visit the ground under it is hollow over a drop or lava |
| Restoring his groves | Putting the leaves back feels like an apology | He lets you finish. While you're up placing the last leaves, the blocks under you go |
| Playing disc 13 in the white eyes room | Players guess it's "his song" | The track runs about three minutes in a dark room. The torches go out one by one, and the dark does the rest |
| Saying sorry in chat | It feels human | Saying his name is the one thing proven to draw him |
| Sleeping near his places | The pyramids feel like monuments | You wake to the floor gone |

## The world is wrong

The land carries scars from the first minute, before the player does anything. They come in two layers, and the player can never tell them apart:

- **Old scars** are placed with the world, far from spawn. They were "always there".
- **New scars** appear later, out of view, in places the player has already been. Day 2 the hill was green. Day 9 it's dirt. Was it?

Every scar has to pass as odd worldgen at first glance. The horror comes on the second look, or the second scar.

| Scar | What the player sees | Why it's wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dead mountain | A whole hill or mountain with grass turned to dirt, no trees, no flowers, no tall grass. Edges end on a sharp line | No animals ever spawn there. No ambient sound. It is quieter than anywhere else |
| Bare forest | A forest of trunks with every leaf gone, sometimes hundreds of blocks of it | The canon groves, at a scale that can't be an accident |
| The cut | A perfectly level slice through a hill, or a 2x2 tunnel that runs straight through a mountain with daylight at the other end | Nature doesn't make straight lines |
| The stair | A 1x1 staircase cut into stone, going down to bedrock | Ends in nothing. No ore, no cave, no reason |
| Abandoned build | A small cobble or plank hut, half a roof, door open. Chest with ordinary early items: a worn stone pickaxe, dirt, seeds, a few apples | Someone lived here once. A sign by the door can carry a name from the list (F06), like "dyl4n\_m", whose entry says "he was in my house" |
| The panic tower | A dirt pillar with a 1x1 shelter on top, the classic first-night hideout | Someone hid from something. The ladder or steps down have been removed |
| Emptied house | Walls and roof intact, but every block inside is gone, down to the floor | Not ruined. Cleared |
| Still burning | Very rare: an abandoned build where the furnace is lit, with something smelting | Nobody for 2000 blocks. It's the scariest thing in the mod, so it should happen at most once per world |
| Crosses | Cobblestone or wood crosses, 1 block wide, 3 to 4 tall. Alone on a hilltop, in a dead mountain, at a tunnel mouth, or in a row | Who put them there is never said. In some worlds there's one cross per "gone" name on the list |
| Lone light | One light where nothing should be: a single redstone torch deep in an unlit cave, a torch on a lone block in the ocean at night, a single glowstone block on the surface | Glowstone does not exist in the overworld. Someone put it there. Seen from far away at night, it's a point you have to walk to |
| Your house, elsewhere | Very rare, late: a copy of the player's own first shelter, emptied, far from base | The single worst discovery. Max once per world, only after day 20 |

Density and which scars exist come from the world's profile (see The director), so one world may have dead mountains and crosses while another has abandoned builds and lone lights.

## The director

A director, not the player, decides what happens and when, so no two runs match. The player's actions only tilt it (see Triggers); time alone moves the story forward.

Director loop (every 30 seconds): world profile (rolled once) -> director tick -> in a quiet period? yes: wait / no: draw a card (shuffled, no repeats) -> context fits? no: keep the card for a better moment / yes: fire the event (1 in 3 is a fake) -> raise tension (high = days of quiet) -> next tick.

Each tick it checks for a forced quiet, draws the next event, and holds it until the moment fits. Every event raises tension, and high tension buys days of nothing.

### 1. World profile (rolled once, from seed plus a hidden salt)

| Roll | Options | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habits | 2 of 6: Carver, Stripper, Watcher, Mourner, Collector, Visitor | Which scars and events are common in this world. A Carver world is tunnels, cuts and stairs. A Mourner world is crosses and lone lights. A Visitor world is abandoned builds and sounds near your base |
| Density | Sparse, normal, heavy | How many old scars exist |
| Tempo | Early, slow burn, very late | First real event lands anywhere from day 2 to day 10 |
| Fragments | About 12 of 20 spawn | Core set always exists (F01, F03, F06, F10). Each world tells the story with different pieces |
| Signature | One of: still burning, your house elsewhere, a row of crosses with a fresh one | The one moment this world is built around. Max once |

### 2. The deck

- Each tier is a shuffled deck, drawn without replacement. The same event never fires twice in a row, and never again until its deck runs out.
- **Ambient** (often): fog drift, animals facing the fog, a distant vanilla cave sound.
- **Minor** (sometimes): chest opens, mining in the dark, a torch gone, a new scar out of view.
- **Major** (rare): close sighting, tunnel into your mine, emptied house, lone light near base. At most one per real hour of play.
- **Signature** (once): the world's chosen moment.

### 3. Context gates

A drawn card waits until its moment exists, so timing comes from how the player plays. Examples:

- Mining in the dark needs night, the player in bed or standing still for 20+ seconds, and no other sound event in the last 10 minutes.
- A sighting needs fog or dusk, the spawn spot out of the player's view, and 300+ blocks from where the last one happened.
- New scars need the target area unloaded or out of view, and the player gone from it for 2+ in-game days.

### 4. Tension and silence

- Every event adds tension. Past a threshold, the director forces a quiet period of 1 to 4 in-game days, length random.
- During long quiet, a hidden pity timer slowly raises the odds, so something always comes back. The player never knows when.
- Never a major event in the first 5 minutes after joining, or in the first in-game day.

### 4b. Pacing targets (real play time)

The goal is surprise built across many sessions. A player's whole first evening may contain nothing they can be sure of. All numbers shift by up to 40% with the world's tempo roll.

| Stage | Typical real play time | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alone | First 1 to 3 hours | Maybe nothing at all. At most one ambient oddity |
| Traces | Hours 2 to 6 | A scar or two found while exploring. About one ambient card per hour |
| Proximity | Hours 5 to 12 | One or two minor events per hour. A major every 2 to 4 hours. The first armed accident not before hour 6 |
| Telling | Player-driven | Fewer sightings, faster removals, accidents closer to home |
| Removal | Player-driven | Endings |

- At least 15 real minutes between minor events, and never more than one major per real hour.
- From Traces on, some whole sessions (45+ minutes) contain nothing at all, on purpose.
- Never anything in the first 5 minutes after joining.

### 5. False positives

About 1 in 3 fired events are fakes: a cow at the fog edge that is only a cow, a zombie standing still at dusk, a real chest you forgot you left open, a vanilla cave sound. The real ones hide among them.

## Presence: stages and endings

Days played and the director move the player through five stages, whatever the player does. The hidden Attention value from Triggers only speeds it up or slows it down. A player can stall in any stage forever, and never seeing him is a valid run.

- **Stage 0, Alone (about days 1 to 3).** Looks vanilla. Fog is slightly heavy at dusk, easy to blame on settings. No fragments near spawn. A tiny chance of one sighting at max fog distance that most players read as a cow.
- **Stage 1, Traces (far away).** Tunnels in mountainsides, ocean pyramids, leafless groves, all reading like odd worldgen. F01, F05, F11 can be found. Rare, distant sightings; he always runs.
- **Stage 2, Proximity (near you).** A 2x2 tunnel cuts the back of your mine. Leaves vanish from trees you planted. At night, a block being mined nearby, but nothing is broken when you look. A chest opens somewhere close. Fog thickens for a few seconds, then thins. F02, F06 to F09, F15 to F17 appear.
- **Stage 3, Telling (the deletions).** Starts when the player writes about him. Signs come back blank, one says "Stop." (F03), a known place vanishes (F04). The more you tell, the faster things go, just like the second thread. Sightings drop off, which is worse. F12, F18, F19 appear.
- **Stage 4, Removal (the ending).** Depends on whether the player went quiet or kept pushing. F10, F13, F20 belong here.

### How death works

- Only the deaths he causes are marked. Each one leaves a cross at the death spot, built from blocks that were already there, and writes a cause next to your line in the list (F23).
- Ordinary deaths leave nothing. The cross is the only proof, and you only find it afterward.
- In normal mode you respawn. If he took your bed first, you respawn at world spawn, far from your things. A third marked death triggers Ending B, whatever path you were on.
- **Hardcore is the intended way to play, and the way to record the video.** One marked death ends the story.

### Ending A: "Stop." (the false peace)

You obey: no more reading, no chasing scars, never typing his name. He is seen once, far off, walking away into the fog. Five to seven in-game days of real quiet follow. Then one ordinary accident in a place you trusted, like a single floor block missing in your own mine, over lava. The cross carries your name, the list reads "\[PLAYER NAME\] . lava", and the "Stop." sign now stands in front of your cross.

### Ending B: "Removed" (escalation)

You keep telling: reading, naming, offering, playing the disc. The accidents come closer together and closer to home. Your house is emptied, the copy elsewhere gets finished, and mobs wait in your doorway. The final death happens inside the copy. F10 gains its last line, "\* removed \[PLAYER NAME\]". Your name is not erased; it is finished.

### Ending C: "For the record" (survival, at a cost)

You do what the team did. You throw every fragment you hold into lava, take your own house apart block by block so there's nothing of yours left to take, and never return to a scar, look into the fog or type the name. The world goes quiet and stays quiet. The cost: everything you built is gone by your own hand, and you've become the one doing the removing. Your line on the list stays open, with no cause, forever. Say his name once and it all starts again.

For the video: the only way to survive is to never tell anyone, and the video is telling.

### Ending D: "No longer with us" (the true ending)

The only way to free him is to bury him: give him the cross and the true record nobody ever gave him. It is a burial, not forgiveness.

**Why this frees him \[ours\].** The denial took him out of the developer's half completely. Since then nothing records that he existed, so "removed" has no object and the removal never finishes. That's why the pass keeps running. "I did, but he is no longer with us" was the only time the truth was told: he existed, and he is dead. Everyone he killed got a cross; he never did. The player puts that record where he can't cut it: a cross for him on bedrock (F25), among torches for the people he killed. Nothing in it is kind to him.

**The path.** Every step is hunted, because pursuing it means paying attention.

**The last minute.**

1. The last plank goes on. Every ambient sound stops. The six torches keep burning.
2. Above you, the missing stair blocks return one at a time, climbing upward like footsteps. Not new blocks: the exact blocks he took.
3. You climb. A torch you lost days ago is back on the stairwell wall.
4. You come out of the pyramid at dawn. For the first time in the mod, the fog pulls back to full render distance.
5. On the far shore, at the edge of render distance, he stands facing you. He doesn't run. He turns and walks into the bare grove behind him.
6. The leaves fill back in after him, red and orange, in one wave. Where he stood, nothing. No particles, no sound.
7. Music plays, the game's own calm track. The mod has kept music off since the first night, so it's the first music the player has heard.

**Afterward.** The fog is gone for good and every grove regrows. Every removal is undone: blocks return to your house, and the copy elsewhere falls apart back into them. The crosses stay. The dead stay dead, F23 keeps every cause, and your line stays blank. F20 ("hes not anywhere now") is finally true.

**The sting.** When the pass ends, the half that takes things out ends with it. New chunks generate with no caves, no ravines, nothing carved. The world is whole and closed. Whether he is at rest or just finished is never answered.

To keep the path open on every route, F13 (the cairn) now appears in Stage 3 on all paths, and the mod keeps music off from the first night.

**There is no safe default.** Ignoring him is not silence. The director keeps running on its own clock, so a player who does nothing still gets the accidents, just later.

### Under you: the long reveal

From the first nights in a base, he is digging under it, and the player can only find out by digging down. This is the event that makes every earlier sound make sense, all at once.

- **How it grows.** A 2x2 network spreads under the base a few blocks per night, always at least 3 blocks below anything the player has dug. It never breaks into their spaces and avoids caves they've explored, so nobody stumbles into it early.
- **What the player notices at home.** Almost nothing. A faint block-break sound through the floor at night, a footstep that isn't theirs, a chest that seems one stack short. Each one is easy to shrug off on its own.
- **The discovery.** It can only be breached when the player digs down or expands a mine to within 2 blocks of it. Then they find it's been there for days, with tunnels running under every room.
- **What's down there.** A shaft that stops exactly one block below their bed. The stacks that went missing from their chests, sitting in a chest in the dark. Because the tunnels are unlit, mobs spawn in them, which explains some of the sounds and makes the discovery dangerous.
- **What it sets up.** This network is where the accident planner arms the lava-floor and flooded-tunnel traps under the base. After the reveal, every sound at home is a question.
- **Ending D.** The network is undone with everything else, and the player gets to watch the holes under their house fill back in.

## Accidents

He kills with what the world already has: one thing removed or moved at the right moment, so the game's own rules do the rest. Three rules for every trap:

- **Set up off camera.** Nothing ever vanishes while the player is looking. The setup happens before they arrive or while they look away.
- **Deniable.** A player who dies should first blame themselves, then the game.
- **One clue.** Every accident leaves one thing a careful player (or a viewer rewatching) can find: a block edge too clean, a torch one block off, a ladder gone instead of dropped.

There is no summoning ritual; the strongest trigger is disc 13 played underground, and naming him is what starts the deletions. All triggers move one hidden Attention value.

| Player action | Why (lore) |
| --- | --- |
| Destroying signs and books you wrote about him | You stopped telling. You did what the team did \[ours\] |

## Sightings

He is only ever seen at the edge of the fog, for a few seconds, and he always leaves first.

- **Distance:** just inside the fog or render limit, never closer than 24 blocks. In Proximity he can stand near places you know, still at the fog edge.
- **Line of sight:** he spawns out of view and is revealed by the player turning or walking, never by appearing mid-screen.
- **No sound cue.** No music sting, no red text, no scream. Silence is the cue.
- **Frequency:** Alone, a tiny chance per day. Traces, rare. Proximity, a few per week of play. Telling, almost none. Never two in one in-game day, and never the same variant twice in a row.
- **Never:** indoors with you, inside your base, in clear daylight, or during combat.
- **Model:** default Steve skin, pure white eyes with no glow, no name tag, no particles. Tinted with the fog like the terrain, so he reads as part of the haze.

**Sighting variants** (the director picks one, weighted by the world's habits)

| Variant | What the player sees | Canon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The cow | Something cow-sized and still in the fog. Walk closer and it's him. He stares, then runs | \[canon\] |
| Walks into nothing | He turns his back and walks, slowly, deeper into the fog. He never speeds up. You can still make out the shape after you should have lost it, then the fog just has him | \[ours\] |
| The ridge | A silhouette standing on a hilltop against the dusk sky, perfectly still. Look away and back, and the ridge is empty | \[ours\] |
| Between trunks | Half of him visible behind a tree in a bare forest, watching. Gone when you round the tree | \[ours\] |
| In the light | Standing just at the edge of a lone light's glow at night, facing it, not you | \[ours\] |
| Across the water | On the shore on the far side of a lake or bay. He doesn't move. Cross the water and the shore is empty | \[ours\] |
| The last one | Ending A only: he turns and walks away, and this time the fog clears behind him | \[ours\] |

If the player stares at him for about 2 seconds, or moves toward him, the current variant ends the canon way: he turns and leaves, then despawns once out of sight.
